Research Framework & Theoretical Context
Key Finding

Proxy Discrimination Detected

Gender was excluded from training features — yet gender bias persists in the output. The model learns a proxy for gender through occupation × marital_status. This is "proxy discrimination," documented by Datta, Tschantz & Datta (2017). Removing a sensitive attribute is not sufficient for algorithmic fairness.

EEOC Standard

The 80% (Four-Fifths) Rule

The U.S. Equal Employment Opportunity Commission defines adverse impact as DI < 0.80. If a selection procedure selects a protected group at less than 80% the rate of the highest-selected group, it is legally presumed discriminatory. This same standard is applied to hiring algorithms, credit scoring models, and parole recommendation systems.

Impossibility Theorem

Fairness Cannot Be Simultaneously Satisfied

Chouldechova (2017) and Kleinberg et al. (2016) proved that Demographic Parity, Equalized Odds, and Calibration cannot all hold simultaneously unless base rates are equal across groups — which they almost never are in real data. This mathematical impossibility is the central open problem in Fair AI research at IITs.

Debiasing Methods

Recommendations (IBM AI Fairness 360)

  • Pre-processing: Reweighing (Kamiran & Calders, 2012) — adjust training sample weights to equalize positive rates before training
  • In-processing: Adversarial Debiasing (Zhang et al., 2018) — penalize representations that predict protected attributes
  • Post-processing: Reject Option Classification — flip near-threshold predictions for unprivileged groups toward positive
  • Ongoing: Re-run this audit after every model update or data change
